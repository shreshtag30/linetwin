How LineTwin works — read this first

A live simulation of a 30-station vehicle assembly line (12 body · 6 paint · 12 final assembly) running right now in a real Python process — nothing here is pre-recorded. Every number updates 8×/second.

  • Bottleneck detection — the Active Period Method names whichever station has been continuously busy the longest (§ Bottleneck).
  • Defect-risk scoring — a trained model scores every station's risk right now from live queue pressure and cycle-time signals (§ Quality & Defects).
  • Sensor-gap inference — 8 of 30 stations have no sensor. Their values are solved live from a graph equation over their neighbours and tagged inferred — N%, never shown as if measured (§ Data Coverage).
  • Rolling-horizon prediction — every second the server forks the line's current state and fast-forwards ~30 simulated minutes to forecast where the bottleneck is headed (§ Trends).
  • Defect genealogy — tracing a flagged defect back to its likely origin station, transfer-delay realigned (§ Quality & Defects).

Try this → in the Quick Control panel, pick a station and a cycle-time multiplier, then Apply. Watch the Bottleneck panel, charts, and Alerts respond within a couple of seconds.

    How this works

    • Active Period Method — every station tracks how long it's been continuously "active" (working, or stopped for a reason that isn't its own fault). The longest current active streak is the bottleneck.
    • Benchmarked against 5 alternatives across three distinct engineered-bottleneck scenarios — active-period methods generalize best overall (90% top-1 across all three), which is why this family is deployed, even though on any single scenario a different comparison method can occasionally edge it out (docs/phases/detector_comparison_multiscenario.csv). The live rule is a momentary variant of that family, not a byte-for-byte replay of the offline benchmark.
    • Confidence — provisional: the gap over the runner-up isn't yet statistically clear. established: it is. Shown as a caveat, never used to suppress or delay detection.
